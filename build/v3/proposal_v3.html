Executive summary

KokoShield Field Desk is a working web app that tells a cocoa scout where to look first after heavy rain. It turns a rain reading into a ranked 30-minute route through the farm's blocks, guides the scout stop by stop on a phone, and lets an agricultural adviser confirm what was found.

Black pod is a rain-driven disease estimated to cost 20 to 30% of world cocoa production each year [25]. After a storm a smallholder has a short window and many blocks, and the usual round often reaches the wettest blocks late. Field Desk replaces that guess with a plan: score each block on slow drainage and previous confirmed cases, choose the highest-risk blocks that fit the time available, and walk them as the shortest loop from the gate.

We will test one question on a Malaysian cocoa farm: in the same 30 minutes, does the ranked route find more confirmed pods per scouting hour than the grower's usual round? Two scouts walk matched groups of blocks after each qualifying rain, and a common follow-up check counts what each route missed.

The problem: the right block, too late

Black pod, caused mainly by Phytophthora palmivora, spreads from infected pods to healthy ones by rain splash [9, 25]. After heavy rain the useful action is simple: find and remove infected pods quickly. The hard part is knowing where to look first.

A smallholder or a single scout has a short window and limited labour. Without a plan, the walk follows memory and habit, so wet, slow-draining blocks with a history of disease may be reached last or not at all. Advisers cannot visit every farm after every storm. Existing tools forecast weather, identify symptoms or keep records, but none turns "it rained heavily" into "walk these blocks first, in this order, in the time you have", or shows whether that advice worked.

The 20-30% figure describes the size of the problem, not the share KokoShield can recover. A confirmed pod is an operational finding; a later harvest check is needed to judge marketable pods saved.

Step 1

Plan: a route that fits the time you have

Figure 4. Field plan after 48 mm of rain on the demo farm (demo data; aerial view AI-generated). The route is A North terraces, D Lower grove, B East rows and G South rows: 4 stops, 30 of 30 min, back to gate.

How the route is built

  1. Trigger. A round is suggested when the nearest public gauge [8] or the grower's own gauge records at least 30 mm in 24 hours.
  2. Score. Each open block earns 3 points for Slow drainage when the trigger is met and 2 points for Confirmed before. Blocks show as Priority, Watch, Routine or Closed.
  3. Fit. Pick the highest-risk blocks whose scouting times fit the budget (15, 20, 30 or 45 minutes).
  4. Order. Walk the chosen blocks as the shortest loop from the gate along the farm's footpaths, and come back to the gate.

What the scout sees

Each stop shows its reason, such as "Slow drainage" and "Confirmed before", so the grower can check the logic. Blocks left out are listed as "Not this round", and closed blocks are excluded rather than scored as zero.

If the rain reading is missing, the app does not guess. It disables the priority route and offers the usual round. The route can be printed as a checklist for a walk without signal.

Before the pilot, the grower and adviser agree the trigger and weights, then freeze them for every scored round.

Step 2

Walk: one stop at a time, on a phone

The Walk screen is built for the field: one block per screen, large buttons, a running clock and a short note on what to check. Every save is stored on the phone first, so a weak signal does not lose a record.

Stop 1 of 4. Block A, North terraces, with its reasons and what to check: lower pods and trunk base.

Found black pod. The scout enters how many pods, here 3, and saves.

Next stop. The record is saved for review and the scout moves to block D, Lower grove.

What each record holds

Block, time, the route that led there, the suspected pod count and an optional note or photo. Each record starts as Pending, so a field observation stays separate from a confirmed finding.

Language and access

The Walk screen has an English and Bahasa Malaysia switch; its wording will be checked by a native speaker before field use. The app runs in a phone browser, with no app store install, and a printed route is the fallback.

Step 3

Prove: confirm cases and count what was found

Scout log

The grower or adviser confirms or rules out each pending case, using one standard for both routes. Only confirmed pods count toward the primary measure. Ruled-out records, such as squirrel damage, stay visible.

Figure 6. Scout log with seeded demo records and reference photos.

Compare

Compare puts the priority route and the usual round side by side under the same time limit: pods per scouting hour, pods found, time to first find and blocks with disease missed.

On the demo farm the screen shows 14 against 4 pods per scouting hour. These are preset finds that demonstrate the method; pilot numbers will come from timed field rounds.

Optional inputs: the Field Node and a drone survey

Neither input is needed for the pilot. Each can add a better signal to the Plan step once it proves it adds information.

Field Node (CAD concept)

Figure 8. The Sensor node page of the prototype, showing the CAD model exploded and a demo feed.

A small enclosure for an air temperature and humidity sensor at block level: an 86 x 62 x 32 mm base, a removable lid and a vented sensor hood, with an ESP32-class controller as a size envelope.

At its true level: it is a CAD design. It measures temperature and humidity only; rainfall needs a separate gauge, and leaf wetness its own sensor or a field record. Power, mounting and outdoor testing are not designed yet. A photo of a 3D print will replace the render once printed. CAD files are attached to this PDF.

Drone survey (borrowed)

Figure 9. Illustrative post-rain flight drawn on the prototype's AI-generated aerial view of the demo farm. Not field data.

A camera drone borrowed from Universiti Malaya maps the blocks once at setup, then flies within 24 hours of a rain trigger, when safe, to mark standing water. That strengthens the Slow drainage signal, here in blocks A, D and G.

What it will not do: detect black pod, which develops on pods under the canopy, or spray. Flights need the grower's written consent and follow current CAAM rules, never in storms. If the drone adds nothing new after two rounds, the pilot drops it and keeps the grower's own observation.

The pilot: one farm, two routes, same 30 minutes

1Malaysian cocoa farm, Selangor
8-12blocks in two matched groups
2scouts, 30 minutes each
4usable paired rounds
6weeks, subject to rain
RM1,200cash budget

The design compares the order in which scouts walk, within the same time budget. Nothing else changes.

The grower's scouted area is divided into 8 to 12 blocks of similar size, split into two groups matched on size, drainage and past cases. Each group holds more blocks than one scout can cover in 30 minutes, so the order of visits matters. In each round one scout follows the KokoShield order in one group and the other follows the grower's usual order in the other. Groups and scouts swap every round.

StageProtocol
Before the roundConfirm the 30 mm trigger, access and the two groups. Record the usual route before showing any recommendation.
Drone check (optional)Within 24 hours, where safe and permitted, note blocks with standing water and compare with the grower's reading.
Two timed routesBoth scouts start together and work 30 minutes each. Walking and recording count toward the time. The first assignment is random.
ConfirmationOne agreed review standard. Where practical, the reviewer does not see which route produced each case.
Follow-up checkOn the same visit, the same systematic check of every eligible block in both groups counts the cases each route missed.

When a round is usable

Both timed walks, eligible blocks, timestamps and the follow-up result are recorded. Rounds with zero cases are kept. If too few rain events occur, we report usability and data coverage and extend the test before claiming performance.

Site and data

Tanjung Sepat, about 1.5 hours from Universiti Malaya, is the candidate site; a farm there is listed by the Kuala Langat council [7], and access is not yet confirmed. The grower owns the record. Maps and photos are shared only with trial participants.

What scouts look for

Lower pods and the trunk base, for dark, spreading patches: the same check the Walk screen shows at every stop. Each suspect pod is counted once, tagged to its block and confirmed or ruled out by the adviser.

How we will measure it

The primary outcome is confirmed pods per scouting hour: newly confirmed diseased pods divided by recorded scouting hours. Speed and misses are measured too, so a route cannot look good by visiting a few obvious blocks.

MeasureCalculationWhy it matters
Confirmed pods per scouting hour (primary)Newly confirmed pods divided by recorded scouting hours, by round and in total.The direct test of the route.
First detectionMinutes to the first observation later confirmed.Earlier removal limits rain-splash spread.
Missed casesCases first found on the common follow-up check, by block.Stops a route winning by concentrating.
Response timeHours from trigger to round; days from confirmation to pod removal.The climate-adaptation link.
UsabilityRoute completion, grower overrides and willingness to rerun.Adoption beyond the pilot.
Harvest observationMarketable pods as a share of pods assessed, where harvest overlaps.Exploratory at one site.

Decision rule for a second site

We go on to a second site only when all three hold:

  1. the ranked route records more confirmed pods per scouting hour in at least 3 of 4 usable rounds (or 75% of usable rounds if the trial is extended);
  2. the follow-up check finds no block missed by the ranked route in two or more rounds; and
  3. the grower says they would use it again.

How we will report

Every paired round with its conditions and the cases verified later, and a count of rounds with improvement, no clear difference and lower performance. One farm can guide a second pilot; a harvest or income claim needs more farms and seasons.

Why the demand is growing

More rain extremes. Heavy wet-season rain is already linked to lower yields in three producing countries [3], so growers face more post-rain decisions with the same labour.

Scattered holdings. Advisers cannot visit every farm after every storm. A cooperative or buyer that reaches many farms through one coordinator is the practical channel [16].

Supply continuity. Buyers that depend on smallholder beans have a reason to fund a low-cost service that protects supply.

Partners, not rivals

KokoShield does not rebuild diagnosis. A scout who needs help with a symptom can use Plantix or a PlantwisePlus factsheet, and the case still goes to the adviser for confirmation.

What could erode the advantage

A diagnosis app could add field routing, or a cooperative could write its own spreadsheet rule. Our defence is evidence and fit: paired results by round, setup a cluster coordinator can manage, and a record sponsors can use. Without field results we claim no advantage.

Next steps

Second site. Repeat the cocoa comparison with a different grower and compare with the first farm. A partner receives the protocol, a data dictionary, results by round and known limitations.

Rice blast in China. The prototype already switches to a rice profile (Hangzhou demo paddy, leaf wetness over an 8-hour trigger) with its own scout log. A field trial needs a local rice partner and adviser; the outcome becomes confirmed affected blocks per scouting hour [11, 14].

Other crops. The block map, ranking, Walk screen and review log transfer; local teams set the crop, disease, weather input and confirmation method, for example grape downy mildew [13] or potato late blight [15].

Shade as a future input. Dense shade worsens black pod, so shade level per block is a credible next signal for the ranking once the pilot rule is proven.

Field Node. Print the enclosure, design power and mounting, and test outdoors before any reading feeds a scored round.
